Fresh Start in Education

Local-Authority Commercial Intelligence Application

Commercial teams were piecing together Local Authority decisions from several Salesforce records. I built one app that shows the relationships, prices, bids and risk in the same place.

AI-Assisted Innovation Salesforce & CRM Data & Analytics Commercial Strategy Automation
Project overview

The app covers 200+ Local Authorities, 1,000+ Accounts and around 2,500 Bids a year. It uses custom objects, AI-assisted Apex, contract information, win/loss analysis, expiry warnings and a scoring model to help teams price work and choose where to focus.

Key result Consolidated 200+ Local Authorities, 1,000+ related Accounts and around 2,500 annual Bids into one dynamic commercial-intelligence application.

01

The fragmented commercial picture

Local Authority information sat in Account, Contract, Bid, Opportunity, Referral and governing-organisation records. Users opened them one by one and assembled the relationship picture themselves. The existing hierarchy did not give operational users a usable one-to-many view.

That was slow and risky. Bespoke prices were sometimes missed, which could contribute to an Account being charged incorrectly. Bid results were also difficult to compare by authority.

  • Siloed commercial and relationship data
  • Manual research across several objects
  • Risk of overlooked custom pricing
  • No consolidated Bid win/loss view
  • Limited basis for regional prioritisation

02

Custom objects and relationship model

I created Local Authority and Bid objects and linked them to Accounts, Opportunities and the custom Contract object. Each Bid records the receiving Account, inherits its parent Local Authority and can become an Opportunity if it succeeds.

Parent-account validation checks the Local Authority relationship. Every summary and score depends on that link being correct.

  • Custom Local Authority object
  • Custom Bid object and Account lookup
  • Opportunity progression for successful Bids
  • Contract-object integration
  • Parent-account hierarchy validation

03

Dynamic relationship and activity summaries

AI-assisted Apex surfaces child Accounts, Master Report Accounts, recent Bid recipients, totals, recent activity and win/loss figures on each authority record. The Master Report view also shows which MATs and other private governing bodies operate in the area.

I adapted the list pattern from another Salesforce app the organisation already owned. Applying similar logic to the custom object avoided more supplier development. The pattern itself was not my original idea.

  • Dynamic child-Account lists
  • Private governing-organisation summaries
  • Total and recent Bid and Referral views
  • Six-month Bid-recipient visibility
  • Consolidated Bid win/loss ratio

04

Contract, pricing and risk visibility

I brought Contract dates, agreement-to-bid status and bespoke prices onto the Local Authority record. A user handling an enquiry can now find the agreed charge rate without interpreting a separate Contract.

The app warns when a Contract has less than 60 days left and marks expired Contracts separately. The summary card shows whether custom pricing exists; the price itself remains on the full record.

  • Contract start and end summaries
  • Agreement-to-bid status
  • Accessible bespoke pricing records
  • 60-day expiry warnings
  • Expired-Contract indicators

05

Validated scoring and dynamic ranking

I designed a score that compares Referrals with the number of Accounts in an authority area. Better-than-proportionate Referral activity raises the score; weaker performance lowers it.

Bid and Referral activity, Account volume and Contract status feed the wider ranking. An active Contract adds weight but is not mandatory, since some valuable authority relationships do not require one. I validated the logic before applying it.

Win/loss ratios help decide where limited bid capacity should go. Some authorities can offer thousands of opportunities in a month, far more than the organisation can submit, so conversion evidence matters.

  • Referral-to-Account performance comparison
  • Bid and Referral activity signals
  • Connected Account influence
  • Active-Contract scoring bonus
  • Validated dynamic card ordering

06

Building the interface with AI assistance

I used Codex and other AI assistance for Apex and visual components. The card interface uses a crown and green, yellow or red flags to show relative value alongside recent activity, win/loss, Contract and pricing status.

I defined the problem, requirements, data model, scoring and build direction, then reviewed, tested and released the output. I do not claim to have written every Apex or visual line unaided. The app is in production and has been refined through use.

  • AI-assisted Apex and component development
  • Human-defined requirements and scoring logic
  • Manual review, testing and validation
  • Dynamic visual ranking and status icons
  • Production rollout and feedback-led refinement

Outcomes

What users see now

One commercial view

One screen shows the authority's relationships, Contracts, prices, Accounts, Bids, Referrals and Opportunities.

Current prioritisation

Scores and rankings refresh with the source data, so the view does not rely on a stale manual assessment.

Reduced pricing risk

Users can find bespoke Contract pricing while handling an enquiry instead of relying on memory.

Reusable commercial evidence

Bid results and relationship scores help direct effort when opportunity volume exceeds capacity.
