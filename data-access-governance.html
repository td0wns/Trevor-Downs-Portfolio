Fresh Start in Education

Salesforce Access Governance, Data Standards and User Adoption

I looked after who could see and change new Salesforce features, then helped more than 30 users put those features into daily work.

Salesforce & CRM Operations Leadership & Stakeholder Management Data & Analytics
Project overview

As the departmental Salesforce super user, I was the first point of contact for access problems, unexpected system behaviour and adoption questions. Development and Referrals used the features daily; Education used them occasionally.

Key result More than 30 users received production access matched to their responsibilities.

01

The governance challenge

New objects, fields and Flows had to reach the people who needed them without opening up unnecessary access. The release could not come at the expense of data quality or process control.

A failure could sit at object, field, record or Flow level. I had to test each release through several user profiles and then support staff while their working habits changed.

02

Role-aligned access controls

I created and maintained permission sets for specific objects, fields and processes. Object permissions controlled who could create, read, edit or delete a record. Field-level security narrowed that access further according to the person's responsibilities.

That included custom operational objects and the Attribution Model. Intended users still needed to complete the relevant Flows and Screen Flows without hitting an access error.

  • Permission sets aligned with departmental responsibilities
  • Controlled standard and custom object access
  • Restricted sensitive or operational fields
  • Enabled required Flow and Screen Flow execution

03

Data and process integrity

I used permissions and validation rules together. Permissions set the boundary; validation caught incomplete or inappropriate changes inside it.

The controls followed real job responsibilities. That kept the system usable without falling back on broad access simply because it was easier to configure.

04

Testing and troubleshooting

Before a wider release, I tested the work under different access levels. I checked that each intended user could see the right data, edit the right records and finish the guided process.

When something failed, I traced the issue through object visibility, field access, record access and Flow execution. Sometimes the fix was configuration; sometimes the user needed a clearer process.

  • Multi-level user-access testing
  • Pre-release validation of custom functionality
  • Object, field, record and Flow diagnosis
  • First-line departmental Salesforce support

05

Making the release usable

I wrote instructions, trained users and stayed available for troubleshooting. The documentation explained the job to be done, not just the Salesforce configuration behind it.

Live feedback exposed details that testing could not. I used those requests and suggestions to refine the first designs as the new features became routine.

Outcomes
