Fresh Start in Education

Salesforce Access Governance, Data Standards and User Adoption

Governed access to Salesforce objects, fields and Flows while protecting data quality and supporting production features available company-wide to more than 30 users.

Salesforce & CRM Operations Leadership & Stakeholder Management Data & Analytics
Project overview

Served as the departmental Salesforce super user and first point of support for access, system behaviour and adoption issues. Production features were available to 30+ users, with daily use concentrated in Development and Referrals and occasional use by Education.

Key result Enabled governed access to production features across more than 30 company users.

01

The governance challenge

New custom objects, fields, Flows and operational processes needed to reach the correct users across the company without granting unnecessary access or weakening data and process controls.

Access issues could occur at object, field, record or Flow level. New functionality therefore needed to be tested from several user perspectives before release, while established users needed clear support as their working practices changed.

02

Role-aligned access controls

Created and maintained permission sets for users who required access to specific Salesforce objects, fields and processes. Object permissions controlled create, read, edit and delete capabilities, while field-level security limited visibility and editing to information required for each responsibility.

This governed access to custom architecture, including operational objects and the Attribution Model object, while ensuring intended users could complete Salesforce Flows and Screen Flows.

  • Permission sets aligned with departmental responsibilities
  • Controlled standard and custom object access
  • Restricted sensitive or operational fields
  • Enabled required Flow and Screen Flow execution

03

Data and process integrity

Used permissions and validation rules together to protect controlled processes and prevent incomplete or inappropriate changes. The aim was to maintain reliable data standards without blocking legitimate day-to-day activity.

This balanced usability with data protection and operational control. Access was based on user responsibilities instead of relying on unrestricted permissions, and safeguards were designed around the way teams actually worked.

04

Testing and troubleshooting

Tested new Salesforce architecture using different user-access levels before wider departmental rollout. This verified that the intended users could see the right objects and fields, work with records and complete guided processes.

Diagnosed and resolved issues involving object visibility, field access, record access, Flow execution and related Salesforce behaviour. Troubleshooting distinguished configuration problems from process or user-guidance needs.

  • Multi-level user-access testing
  • Pre-release validation of custom functionality
  • Object, field, record and Flow diagnosis
  • First-line departmental Salesforce support

05

Adoption through practical support

Supported controlled rollout through documentation, user guidance, training and ongoing troubleshooting. Technical configuration was translated into clear departmental processes so users understood how new objects, fields, Flows and controls affected their daily work.

Combining secure configuration with practical enablement reduced access-related disruption and helped new CRM architecture become part of established working practices. Initial designs were also refined through requests, suggestions and feedback from live use.

Outcomes

What the governance model enabled

Company-wide feature access

More than 30 users could access production features, with permissions matched to their responsibilities and daily adoption concentrated in two core teams.

Protected data quality

Permissions and validation rules worked together to preserve controlled processes and prevent inappropriate changes.

More reliable releases

Testing across different access levels identified usability and configuration issues before broader departmental rollout.

Supported user adoption

Documentation, training and troubleshooting connected technical change with clear, usable day-to-day working practices.
