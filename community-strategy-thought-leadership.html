01

The strategic question

Conventional funnels often describe an isolated prospect moving toward a transaction. The work began from a different question: how do trust, familiarity and engagement move through connected people, and how can organisations design around those relationships?

Downs Community Consulting became the platform for developing that position through research, writing and framework design. It remains a founder-led side venture supported by a mentor and adviser, with its value demonstrated through published work and the ideas later applied within employed systems delivery.

  • Relationship-led commercial strategy
  • Community as an interconnected system
  • Trust and familiarity across buying relationships
  • Practical application to marketing and change
  • Clear separation between research and client outcomes

02

Research and synthesis

Reviewed more than 20 community-funnel models drawn from charitable and digital communities. The source models ranged from three to nine stages, requiring comparison across different language, goals and definitions of participation.

Supplemented the model review with an expert interview involving a financial representative with more than 20 years of experience. The research was used as evidence for framework development, while third-party observations remained distinct from outcomes claimed for the venture itself.

  • 20+ comparative framework reviews
  • Charitable and digital community models
  • Expert interview design and interpretation
  • Cross-model pattern identification
  • Evidence boundaries for published claims

03

An original five-stage community funnel

Synthesised the research into five stages: Awareness, Interaction, Engagement, Buy-In and Advocacy. Trust and quality operate across every stage rather than appearing as a single final-state outcome.

The framework also proposes that trust, familiarity and engagement can transfer through relationships. That creates a more realistic model for complex buying environments, where one person's experience can influence colleagues, families, organisations and wider communities.

  • Awareness
  • Interaction
  • Engagement
  • Buy-In
  • Advocacy

04

A community taxonomy for organisational use

Developed a taxonomy covering close, broad, digital and product communities across both internal and external organisational settings. The structure gives teams a clearer way to identify which relationships exist, what kind of participation they support and where influence can travel.

Elements of the relationship-led philosophy later informed employed work at Fresh Start in Education, including the way connected Accounts, buying committees and community influence were represented in commercial systems and marketing journeys.

  • Close and broad communities
  • Digital and product communities
  • Internal and external applications
  • Buying-committee and relationship context
  • Transfer from strategic idea to systems thinking

05

Published community and MCAE thought leadership

Authored and published a seven-part community-strategy blog series and four distinct MCAE pieces: a three-role overview and papers on predictive architecture, self-healing systems and governed intelligent orchestration.

Used LinkedIn and website engagement evidence to refine the messaging and build professional connections. The work combines commercial strategy, technical communication and systems thinking while keeping conceptual future-state architecture distinct from functionality deployed in an employer environment.

  • Seven-part community-strategy series
  • Four MCAE thought-leadership pieces
  • Technical ideas translated for business readers
  • Engagement-informed message refinement
  • Clear boundaries between concept and implementation
