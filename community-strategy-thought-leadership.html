Downs Community Consulting

Community Strategy Framework and MCAE Thought Leadership

I started Downs Community Consulting to examine a question that kept appearing in my work: how does trust move between people, and what should an organisation do with that knowledge?

Commercial Strategy Marketing Salesforce & CRM Data & Analytics Leadership & Stakeholder Management
Project overview

Through the side venture, I developed a five-stage community funnel and a taxonomy for different kinds of community. I also published seven community-strategy blogs and four papers on MCAE.

01

The strategic question

A conventional funnel follows one prospect towards a transaction. Real decisions are often less tidy. Trust and familiarity can travel through colleagues, families and communities before the person who finally buys ever appears in the data.

DCC gave me somewhere to test that idea through research and writing. It remains my side venture, supported by a mentor and adviser. Its evidence is the published work and the ideas I later applied in employment, not completed client engagements.

  • Relationship-led commercial strategy
  • Community as an interconnected system
  • Trust and familiarity across buying relationships
  • Practical application to marketing and change
  • Clear separation between research and client outcomes

02

Research and synthesis

I compared more than 20 funnels from charitable and digital communities. They ranged from three to nine stages and used very different language for participation, loyalty and action.

I also interviewed a financial representative with more than 20 years of experience. That interview informed the model. I do not present the interviewee's observations as results achieved by DCC.

  • 20+ comparative framework reviews
  • Charitable and digital community models
  • Expert interview design and interpretation
  • Cross-model pattern identification
  • Evidence boundaries for published claims

03

An original five-stage community funnel

The research became five stages: Awareness, Interaction, Engagement, Buy-In and Advocacy. Trust and quality sit across all five; they are not rewards that appear only at the end.

The model also allows influence to travel between people. One person's experience can affect a colleague, family, organisation or wider community, which better reflects a buying committee than a single isolated journey.

  • Awareness
  • Interaction
  • Engagement
  • Buy-In
  • Advocacy

04

A community taxonomy for organisational use

I grouped communities as close, broad, digital or product-based, with internal and external versions of each. The taxonomy helps identify the relationship involved and the kind of participation it can support.

That thinking later influenced my Fresh Start work, including how I represented account families, buying committees and community influence in Salesforce and marketing journeys.

  • Close and broad communities
  • Digital and product communities
  • Internal and external applications
  • Buying-committee and relationship context
  • Transfer from strategic idea to systems thinking

05

Eleven published pieces

I wrote a seven-part community-strategy series and four MCAE pieces: a three-role overview plus papers on predictive architecture, self-healing systems and intelligent orchestration.

Reader response on LinkedIn and the website helped me refine the message and build professional connections. Conceptual MCAE ideas remain clearly separate from functions I have deployed for an employer.

  • Seven-part community-strategy series
  • Four MCAE thought-leadership pieces
  • Technical ideas translated for business readers
  • Engagement-informed message refinement
  • Clear boundaries between concept and implementation
