Fresh Start in Education

Local-Authority Account Hierarchy, Contract Pricing and Commercial Risk

I reorganised Salesforce around a commercial fact the old structure missed: one Local Authority can influence the pricing, funding or access conditions of many otherwise separate customers.

Salesforce & CRM Automation Data & Analytics Commercial Strategy Operations
Project overview

I made the case for treating every Local Authority as a Salesforce Account and placing organisations in its area beneath it. Flows and validation rules now maintain that hierarchy across 1,000+ Accounts and 20,000+ Leads and Contacts. Contract and Opportunity automation also puts bespoke pricing where users can find it.

Key result Applied across 1,000+ Accounts and 20,000+ Leads and Contacts, representing 200+ local authorities.

01

The commercial problem

Every customer sat within a Local Authority area, but a location field could not describe the commercial relationship. Depending on the area, the authority might set prices, fund or commission services, issue projects, restrict provider access or take little active role.

Salesforce held individual relationships, but users could not open one authority and see every client affected by it. That made contract-based communication, pricing checks and authority-level risk harder to manage.

  • Authorities could set pricing, fund, commission or restrict service access.
  • Geography alone could not represent commercial and contractual influence.
  • Bespoke pricing could be overlooked within siloed Contract records.
  • No consolidated way to prioritise authority relationships by exposure.

02

A local-authority-centred account model

I proposed a Local Authority Account for each area, used as the parent of every relevant organisation. This gave the authority a real place in the account model instead of leaving it as a piece of geography.

The model covers more than 1,000 Salesforce Accounts and 20,000 Leads and Contacts across 200+ Local Authorities.

  • 200+ local-authority Accounts
  • 1,000+ related organisation Accounts
  • 20,000+ leads and contacts
  • Parent-account hierarchy across all Accounts

03

Metadata-driven assignment and governance

I put assignment and ownership rules into Custom Metadata Types so they could be maintained without rewriting the automation. Flows apply the Local Authority to Accounts, Contacts and Leads. That context is available in MCAE before and after Lead conversion.

Validation rules allow only approved Local Authority Accounts to be selected as parents. They also prevent a new organisation Account from being saved without one.

  • Custom Metadata Type-driven Flows
  • Account ownership assignment logic
  • Contact and Lead local-authority attribution
  • Parent-account eligibility validation rules
  • Mandatory parent validation on account creation

04

Contract-based segmentation

MCAE can now segment by authority contract or commercial relationship, not just county. Messages can reach the organisations affected by a particular price, contract, commissioning rule or service restriction.

The same structure shows how many Accounts an authority can influence and how commercially important they are. That gives relationship planning a firmer basis than local knowledge alone.

  • Contract- and authority-based MCAE segmentation
  • Bespoke messaging by pricing or commissioning context
  • Authority-level influence analysis
  • Relationship prioritisation by commercial exposure

05

Commercial concentration risk

One poor authority relationship can affect many independent Accounts in the same area. Oxfordshire was a useful example. The authority applied custom pricing but was otherwise fairly hands-off. Because the service was popular locally, a deterioration in that one relationship still represented substantial exposure.

Making that exposure visible helped the team decide which authority relationships deserved active attention.

  • Authority-level exposure across related accounts
  • Oxfordshire custom-pricing example
  • Relationship-risk visibility for account planning

06

Contract pricing and Opportunity automation

I extended the Contract object to hold authority-specific prices, then used Flow to bring the applicable price into an Opportunity. Sales and enquiry teams no longer had to rely on memory when preparing a quote.

Pricing covers 200+ authorities and roughly half as many distinct contractual arrangements. Opportunity values can now use the relevant contract price instead of a generic assumption.

  • Extended Contract object with bespoke pricing
  • Automated Opportunity pricing Flows
  • Sales quoting support
  • Improved revenue forecasting accuracy
