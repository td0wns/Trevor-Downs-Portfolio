Personal

I have always liked taking things apart to understand them.

I grew up in rural Idaho, where limited tools, time or capacity often meant finding a workable path rather than waiting for ideal conditions. Resourcefulness was simply part of life long before I thought of it as a professional strength.

That instinct still shows up in the things I enjoy: building or rebuilding websites, LEGO sets and motorcycles; getting outdoors for a challenging hill walk, a surf or a camping trip; and getting absorbed in the ideas that connect history, philosophy, different cultures and a good story. Having lived in both the United States and England, I’ve learned to look for the useful parts of different perspectives. I’m equally happy getting nerdy over the details or finding very good coffee.

Professional

Most of my work starts with something that does not quite work.

I start by asking who is affected, what they are trying to do and why the current setup gets in their way. Only then do I decide whether the answer belongs in technology, process, communication or some mix of the three. Recently that has meant hands-on Salesforce and marketing automation work alongside operational design and company-wide change.

At Fresh Start in Education, I identify needs, shape solutions, secure approval, deploy them into production and remain accountable for how they perform in practice. The systems I have developed are available across a company of 30+ users, with daily use concentrated in two core teams. Through Downs Community Consulting, I also publish research-led community-strategy and MCAE frameworks.

Earlier in my career I delivered events in Australia, the United States and the United Kingdom for different organisations. Events taught me how quickly people and operations affect each other. Community research gave me a way to think about trust and relationships. CRM and automation gave me somewhere to apply both lessons.

How I bring them together

The same habits follow me into work.

The instinct that makes me want to rebuild something is the same one that makes me question a clumsy process. I want to know what it is meant to do, why it behaves as it does and whether the problem needs a repair or a rethink.

I tend to think for a while, test carefully and then move quickly once the shape of the answer is clear. I am stubborn about worthwhile problems, but not precious about my first idea. The point is to give people useful tools and enough context to exercise their own judgement.

“I wish whoever made this would make a better one, then retire.”

A saying from my great-grandfather. It is a memorable expression of the urge to take responsibility for improving what falls short.
