Personal

I like understanding how things work—and making them work better.

I grew up in rural Idaho, where limited tools, time or capacity often meant finding a workable path rather than waiting for ideal conditions. Resourcefulness felt normal long before I thought of it as a professional strength.

That instinct still shows up in the things I enjoy: building or rebuilding websites, LEGO sets and motorcycles; getting outdoors for a challenging hill walk, a surf or a camping trip; and getting absorbed in the ideas that connect history, philosophy, different cultures and a good story. Having lived in both the United States and England, I’ve learned to look for the useful parts of different perspectives. I’m equally happy getting nerdy over the details—or finding very good coffee.

Professional

I turn complex problems into practical systems.

I start with the people affected and the outcome they need, then work out how technology, process and commercial thinking can make that outcome possible. My recent work combines hands-on Salesforce and marketing automation delivery with strategy, operational design and company-wide implementation.

At Fresh Start in Education, I identify needs, shape solutions, secure approval, deploy them into production and remain accountable for how they perform in practice. The systems I have developed are available across a company of 30+ users, with daily use concentrated in two core teams. Through Downs Community Consulting, I also publish research-led community-strategy and MCAE frameworks.

My career has taken me through event delivery in Australia, the United States and the United Kingdom with different organisations. Events developed my understanding of people and operations; community research gave me a framework for understanding trust and relationships; CRM and automation gave me tools to put that thinking into practice.

How I bring them together

Curiosity finds the connections. Ownership turns them into results.

The personal and professional sides of me are not separate. The same instinct that makes me want to rebuild something also makes me ask what a system is meant to do, why it works the way it does and whether there is a better route to the outcome.

I think deeply before I act, experiment methodically and stay with a worthwhile problem until the solution works in the real world. But the work always comes back to people: giving them useful tools, respecting their judgement and making change practical enough to adopt. That is how resourcefulness becomes strategy, and how strategy becomes a system people can actually use.

“I wish whoever made this would make a better one, then retire.”

A family saying from my great-grandfather—and a memorable expression of the urge to take responsibility for improving what falls short.
