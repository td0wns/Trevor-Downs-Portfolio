Fresh Start in Education

Initial MCAE Audience, Consent and Suppression Architecture

I built the first set of rules that decides who can enter an MCAE audience, who must be suppressed and which records can qualify again after their data changes.

Marketing Automation Salesforce & CRM Data & Analytics Operations Implementation & Change
Project overview

About 400 lists manage qualification, suppression, subscription preferences, data repair and segmentation. A prospect can move into or out of an audience when their data, permission or engagement changes.

Key result About 400 MCAE lists manage three commercial audiences and allow prospects to requalify when their data changes.

01

The audience-management challenge

The new MCAE instance needed a dependable way to separate sendable prospects from unsubscribes, suppressed records, disengaged people and data that still needed repair.

Three commercial audiences had different rules and buying patterns. Manually maintained lists would fall out of date as soon as a prospect's data or subscription changed.

  • Multiple audiences with distinct commercial purposes
  • Changing permission and engagement states
  • Incomplete business and geographic data
  • Risk of broken merge tags in active sends
  • Need for continuous, automatic requalification

02

Dynamic qualification and list architecture

I separated current state from history. Dynamic lists answer questions that can change, such as whether the data is clean or the prospect is still engaged. Static micro-segmentation lists keep actions that happened in the past.

To qualify, a prospect needs a name, an email address, the right subscription and no place on the Master Suppression List. Membership updates automatically when any of those inputs changes.

  • Dynamic lists for current prospect states
  • Static lists for historical actions
  • Minimum viable audience requirements
  • Automated entry and removal
  • Continuous prospect requalification

03

Suppression, remediation and data recovery

I created one dynamic Master Suppression List from smaller issue lists. It covers unsubscribes, do-not-email flags, hard bounces, blank first names, duplicates, test records, messaging fatigue and other reasons not to send.

Not every problem deserves permanent exclusion. Correctable records leave the active audience and enter a repair route. Separate misfit lists expose missing business-type or Local Authority data. Once fixed, those prospects can qualify again without manual re-entry.

  • Dynamic Master Suppression List
  • Issue-specific suppression controls
  • Misfit and geographic-misfit lists
  • Data correction and enrichment workflows
  • Automatic return to eligible audiences

04

Consent and subscription governance

I built branded preference and unsubscribe pages with cascading rules. A prospect can leave one audience without losing every valid subscription. A universal unsubscribe removes them from every list that can send email.

Enquiry and gated-content forms record where an opt-in came from. When systems disagree, the most recently updated subscription state takes precedence.

  • Branded preference and unsubscribe pages
  • Audience-level opt-out controls
  • Universal unsubscribe compliance
  • Subscription-source tracking
  • Latest-state subscription governance

05

Three-audience segmentation model

I organised MCAE around provision and services, a community newsletter and professional development for schools. A prospect can belong to more than one, but must meet each audience's own requirements.

Salesforce supplies business type and Local Authority data for primary schools, secondary schools, academies and authority teams. Formula fields derive region and county, removing much of the manual geographic maintenance.

  • Provision and services audience
  • Community and buying-committee audience
  • Professional-development audience
  • Business-type and client-archetype logic
  • Salesforce-informed geographic segmentation

06

Drawing a line under the first build

I completed the initial production architecture in January 2026. At that point, all three audiences had working qualification rules, controls and data routes.

From February, I treated monitoring and optimisation as a separate phase. That later work includes expiring scores, reversible profiles, certainty states and human review.

  • Completed initial architecture
  • Production handoff in January 2026
  • Three governed commercial audiences
  • Clear boundary between build and optimisation
  • Foundation for ongoing lifecycle governance

Outcomes
