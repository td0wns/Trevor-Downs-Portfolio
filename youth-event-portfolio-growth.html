Life Church

Youth Programme Revitalisation, Audience Growth and Cost Control

Revitalised a youth and community event portfolio through stronger programming, grassroots marketing, stakeholder partnerships, CRM-informed engagement, operational redesign and disciplined resource management.

Events & Engagement Marketing Operations Leadership & Stakeholder Management
Project overview

Owned departmental event strategy and execution across in-person, digital and hybrid programmes, combining audience development, marketing, team leadership, registration, follow-up and post-event improvement. Tripled recurring average attendance, doubled the community-member base and reduced annual departmental spending by 30% while maintaining event quality.

Key result Tripled recurring average attendance, doubled the community-member base and reduced annual departmental spending by 30% while maintaining delivery quality.

01

The programme challenge

The youth department needed a clearer event strategy, stronger delivery systems and more relevant audience engagement. Several events and programmes had stalled, while live activity still needed to continue for youth and community audiences.

Improvement required more than isolated event promotion. Programming, operations, marketing, registration, follow-up, stakeholder relationships and resource use all needed to work as one repeatable portfolio.

  • Stalled events requiring renewed momentum
  • Youth and vulnerable community audiences
  • Several in-person and digital formats
  • Inconsistent operational processes
  • Need for sustained rather than one-off growth

02

Portfolio strategy and programme redesign

Reviewed the event portfolio and developed a stronger departmental roadmap spanning in-person, digital and hybrid delivery. Programming was refined to improve relevance, consistency and the connection between individual events.

Agile-style growth planning supported ongoing adjustment while live programmes continued. Stalled activity was revitalised, contributing to a 15% increase in the event portfolio and stronger systems for repeatable delivery.

  • Departmental event strategy
  • Youth programme roadmap
  • In-person, digital and hybrid formats
  • Revitalisation of stalled activity
  • 15% event-portfolio growth

03

Grassroots marketing and audience development

Combined social media, email, calls, text messaging, printed collateral and in-person outreach to improve awareness and participation. Campaign messaging and content were shaped around the needs of young people, families and the surrounding community.

Growth activity tripled recurring average attendance, doubled the community-member base and achieved peak attendance growth of 45% for an individual event, creating stronger momentum across the portfolio.

  • Social and email campaign activity
  • Calls, texts and printed outreach
  • Audience-specific event messaging
  • Tripled recurring average attendance
  • 45% peak growth for an individual event

04

Relationships, registration and follow-up

Strengthened relationships with participants, families and partner organisations, connecting community trust with event planning and delivery. Registration and follow-up workflows helped maintain contact beyond the moment of attendance.

CRM-informed engagement and targeted communication supported the doubled community-member base and supported stronger community-member satisfaction.

  • Participant and family relationships
  • Community stakeholder partnerships
  • Registration workflows
  • CRM-informed follow-up
  • Community-member satisfaction insight

05

Analysis and continuous improvement

Used post-event analysis and weekly data review to identify what was working, refine programme decisions and improve future delivery. Attendance, engagement and operational evidence informed practical adjustments rather than remaining retrospective reporting.

This cycle supported more consistent event experiences, lower costs and shorter planning and execution cycles as the portfolio expanded.

  • Post-event review cycles
  • Weekly performance analysis
  • Data-informed programme decisions
  • Lower costs and shorter delivery cycles
  • Repeatable operational improvement

06

Cost control without reducing quality

Reduced annual departmental spending by 30% through smarter purchasing, recycling initiatives, improved resource management and redesigned workflows. The saving came from operational efficiency work alongside the programme-growth activity.

Event and product quality was maintained while departmental processes were restructured to improve consistency and support ongoing campaign and programme delivery.

  • Smarter purchasing decisions
  • Recycling and resource-use initiatives
  • Workflow and process redesign
  • 30% reduction in annual departmental spending
  • Maintained event quality

Outcomes

What the revitalised programme delivered

Sustained audience growth

Tripled recurring average attendance and doubled the community-member base through stronger programming, marketing and audience relationships.

Revived event portfolio

Restarted stalled programmes, expanded the portfolio by 15% and created clearer systems for consistent in-person, digital and hybrid delivery.

Expanded team capacity

Led 10 rotating established volunteers and recruited and trained nine additional junior volunteers, matching responsibilities to their experience.

More efficient operations

Reduced annual departmental spending by 30% through resource and workflow improvements while maintaining event quality.
