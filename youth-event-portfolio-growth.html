Life Church

Youth Programme Revitalisation, Audience Growth and Cost Control

I took on a youth programme that had lost momentum, restarted stalled activity and changed how the department planned, promoted and paid for its events.

Events & Engagement Marketing Operations Leadership & Stakeholder Management
Project overview

I owned the event plan and delivery across in-person, digital and hybrid programmes. Recurring attendance tripled, the community member base doubled and annual departmental spending fell by 30% without reducing event quality.

Key result Tripled recurring average attendance, doubled the community-member base and reduced annual departmental spending by 30% while maintaining delivery quality.

  • Budget and operational control

01

The programme challenge

Several youth events and programmes had stalled. The department still had live activity to run, but no clear plan for rebuilding the wider portfolio.

Promotion alone would not fix it. The programme, registration, follow-up, partner relationships and use of resources all needed attention.

  • Stalled events requiring renewed momentum
  • Youth and vulnerable community audiences
  • Several in-person and digital formats
  • Inconsistent operational processes
  • Need for sustained rather than one-off growth

02

Portfolio strategy and programme redesign

I reviewed every event and set a roadmap for in-person, digital and hybrid activity. I changed the programming to make individual events more relevant and the overall calendar more coherent.

Plans changed as I learned from live delivery. Restarting stalled activity contributed to a 15% increase in the event portfolio.

  • Departmental event strategy
  • Youth programme roadmap
  • In-person, digital and hybrid formats
  • Revitalisation of stalled activity
  • 15% event-portfolio growth

03

Grassroots marketing and audience development

I used social media, email, calls, texts, print and face-to-face outreach. The message changed according to whether I was speaking to a young person, a family or the wider community.

Recurring average attendance tripled and the community-member base doubled. One event recorded peak attendance growth of 45%.

  • Social and email campaign activity
  • Calls, texts and printed outreach
  • Audience-specific event messaging
  • Tripled recurring average attendance
  • 45% peak growth for an individual event

04

Relationships, registration and follow-up

I spent time on relationships with participants, families and partner organisations. Their feedback informed the programme, while registration and follow-up kept contact going after an event.

CRM information helped target communication and keep track of the growing community base.

  • Participant and family relationships
  • Community stakeholder partnerships
  • Registration workflows
  • CRM-informed follow-up
  • Community-member satisfaction insight

05

Analysis and continuous improvement

I reviewed data weekly and examined each event afterwards. Attendance, engagement and operational problems then informed the next programme decision.

The feedback loop shortened planning cycles, lowered costs and made delivery more consistent as the portfolio grew.

  • Post-event review cycles
  • Weekly performance analysis
  • Data-informed programme decisions
  • Lower costs and shorter delivery cycles
  • Repeatable operational improvement

06

Cost control without reducing quality

I cut annual departmental spending by 30% through better purchasing, recycling, resource management and workflow changes.

The saving came from how the work was organised, not from reducing the quality of the events or products.

  • Smarter purchasing decisions
  • Recycling and resource-use initiatives
  • Workflow and process redesign
  • 30% reduction in annual departmental spending
  • Maintained event quality

What changed for the department

Sustained audience growth

More efficient operations

Annual spending fell by 30% while event quality was maintained.
